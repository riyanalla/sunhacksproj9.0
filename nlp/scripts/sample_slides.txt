STA 427: Bayesian Statistics
Lecture 1: What Is Bayesian Statistics?

Two Interpretations of Probability
• Frequentist interpretation: probability is the limit of the relative frequency of an event over many trials
• Bayesian interpretation: probability is a degree of belief in an event
• The degree of belief can change when we observe new data

Prior Knowledge
• Prior knowledge comes from previous experiments, expert opinion, or personal beliefs
• We encode prior knowledge as a prior distribution over the unknown parameter
• Example: a coin with unknown probability of heads, prior distribution Beta(2, 2)

Bayes' Theorem
• posterior distribution ∝ likelihood × prior distribution
• The likelihood describes how probable the observed data is for each parameter value
• The posterior distribution combines prior knowledge with the data

Frequentist vs Bayesian
• Frequentist methods: parameters are fixed, data are random
• Bayesian methods: parameters are random, and we update beliefs with data
• Frequentist confidence interval vs Bayesian credible interval

Updating Beliefs
• Start with a prior distribution
• Observe data and compute the likelihood
• Apply Bayes' theorem to get the posterior distribution
• Today's posterior becomes tomorrow's prior
